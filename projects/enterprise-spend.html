My approach

Separate the data boundaries

I kept real DEFRA profiling separate from the fictional corporate analytics path. This prevents synthetic findings from being presented as public-data facts.

Govern the transformation

I used stable keys, MySQL controls, dbt models, tests, and reporting marts to keep business logic traceable.

Test and observe the complete path

I used Airflow, reconciliation, freshness checks, and repeat loads before reporting. The Power BI monitoring page, Grafana, and Streamlit read the same governed monitoring views.

Business impact

Made spend-control risk visible before action.

£35.53m

Real DEFRA duplicates created a 0.62% sensitivity in the published total. The analysis exposed this risk before supplier or concentration conclusions.

The fictional scenario also prioritised £116.95m without an active matching contract and £44.35m linked to seven high-risk vendors.

Evidence boundary: the £35.53m sensitivity uses real public data. Corporate control findings are deterministic fictional results, not realised savings.

The problem

Trust must survive every transformation.

Spend data is useful only when users can trust its source, transformation, and totals.

Data quality

Duplicate, incomplete, or inconsistent records can change financial totals.

Pipeline reliability

Repeated loads must not create duplicate records.

Operational visibility

A failed or stale load must be visible before users act on the reporting output.

Design decisions

The design protects meaning.

Each decision controls a specific risk in the path from source data to reporting.

01

Why dbt marts feed Power BI

Business logic stays in the governed data layer. Power BI does not recreate the same logic.

02

Why duplicate records are flagged

A duplicate can contain valid business information. The pipeline does not remove it without evidence.

03

Why the load is idempotent

A rerun must not change record counts or financial totals. Explore idempotency →

04

Why freshness is separate from business date

Historical data can be valid even when its business date is old. The monitor tests load recency and period completeness as separate controls. Explore data freshness →

Decision evidence

Question → visual → decision.

Business and monitoring views each have one decision job. Every screenshot is evidence, not decoration.

01 · Portfolio control

Where is spend concentrated?

Why this visual exists. The KPI row, time trend, currency mix, and ranked bars separate scale, movement, and concentration.

Finding

£1.16bn spans 5,000 expenses. Contract compliance is 73.20%, and October has the highest monthly spend at £115.55m.

Interpretation

The overview identifies where to investigate. It does not prove vendor risk or control failure by itself.

Decision

Move from the portfolio view to contract and vendor controls before setting an intervention.

02 · Contract control

Which control gap needs priority?

Why this visual exists. Exposure by department and category is shown with vendor risk, compliance, and a review watchlist.

Finding

£305.92m is non-compliant spend. No active matching contract is the largest cause at £116.95m. Seven high-risk vendors account for £44.35m.

Interpretation

Non-compliance is not one problem. Contract coverage and high-risk vendor performance require different controls.

Decision

Prioritise contract matching and coverage, then review the seven high-risk vendors separately.

03 · Operations

Where does approval fail?

Why this visual exists. Duration, SLA breaches, priority, and department are combined to separate slow flow from service failure.

Finding

Procurement has the longest mean approval cycle at 64.04 hours. Research has the highest SLA breach rate at 42.04%.

Interpretation

The departments show different failure patterns. Mean duration and breach rate are not interchangeable.

Decision

Investigate workflow delay in Procurement and SLA control in Research. Do not apply one response to both.

04 · Reporting readiness

Is the latest recorded output ready for reporting?

Why this visual exists. The Power BI monitoring page places load recency, rule coverage, recent runs, and rule-level evidence beside the governed business report.

Finding

The latest recorded check passed all six freshness rules with zero failures and a 0.00-hour load age.

Interpretation

The page confirms reporting readiness at the recorded check time. It is an imported audit snapshot, not a live service guarantee.

Decision

Confirm the six control results before using the business pages. Use Grafana when near-real-time operational refresh is required.

05 · Pipeline health

Is the latest pipeline output safe to use?

Why this visual exists. Grafana combines overall status, load age, rule results, recent runs, and failure history in one operational view.

Finding

The first live setup check detected an operational load age of 243.662 hours. After a controlled reload, the latest run passed all six rules with zero failures.

Interpretation

The monitor detects stale delivery without treating the fixed historical business period as a live-feed failure.

Decision

Pause use when a critical rule fails. Refresh the controlled load, rerun the checks, and confirm recovery before reporting continues.

06 · Rule evidence

Can an analyst inspect the same controls without Grafana?

Why this visual exists. Streamlit provides a lightweight Python view of the same governed MySQL monitoring contract.

Finding

The application query returned PASS, six passed rules, zero failed rules, and six rule-level evidence rows.

Interpretation

Grafana and Streamlit do not calculate freshness independently. Both read four shared, read-only MySQL views.

Decision

Use Grafana for continuous local operations and Streamlit for transparent inspection and demonstration.

Implementation proof · Python

Verify the rerun, not only the first load.

The loader runs twice and compares row counts. It stops the pipeline if a rerun changes the result.

View implementation on GitHub ↗ 
first_counts = load_once(connection, project_root)
report = {"first_run_counts": first_counts}
if args.verify_idempotency:
    second_counts = load_once(connection, project_root)
    report["second_run_counts"] = second_counts
    report["idempotency_verified"] = first_counts == second_counts
    if not report["idempotency_verified"]:
        raise RuntimeError("Row counts changed on rerun")
