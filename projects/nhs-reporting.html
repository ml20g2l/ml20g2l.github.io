NHS Workforce Capacity & Service Delivery Reporting

A repeatable monthly reporting process for workforce, availability, demand, and delivery performance.

View system ↓ View GitHub ↗
Role Data Analyst · Analytics Engineer
Tools Power Query · Excel · Power BI
Scope April 2024–March 2025
Cohort 20 London providers
Outcome Repeatable reporting cycle

My approach

Treat each month as a controlled intake

I applied deterministic file precedence and recorded source provenance for every reporting cycle.

Keep exceptions visible

I classified rows as Accepted, Review Required, or Rejected and reconciled totals across sources.

Serve governed management views

I used a KPI dictionary, star schema, Excel PivotTables, and Power BI views for repeatable use.

Business impact

Reduced recurring report preparation work.

≈80%

The governed refresh process replaced repeated manual preparation while preserving source lineage, KPI definitions, and exception traceability.

Evidence boundary: this is a controlled portfolio workflow using public aggregate data. It is not a live NHS production deployment.

The problem

Monthly reporting must control change.

A new file can contain revised, duplicate, or invalid records. Managers still need consistent KPI outputs.

Input control

Select the correct file version and preserve source provenance.

Data quality

Classify records as Accepted, Review Required, or Rejected.

Decision use

Give non-technical managers a stable operational view.

Decision evidence

What needs management attention?

Three views move from portfolio status to service pressure and then to reporting trust.

01 · Executive overview

Where is performance off track?

Why this visual exists. Workforce, demand, delivery, pressure, and provider alerts are visible in one management view.

Finding

March demand rose 12.6% from February while four-hour performance was 76.5%, 1.5 percentage points below the reference.

Interpretation

The headline connects rising activity to delivery pressure, while the alert table identifies providers for review.

Decision

Move to provider-level demand and long-wait analysis before assigning operational action.

02 · Demand and delivery

Which providers need review?

Why this visual exists. Demand, admissions, four-hour breaches, and 12-hour waits are shown together so volume and service failure are not confused.

Finding

Lewisham and Greenwich has the lowest four-hour performance at 65.7%. Barts has the largest over-four-hour count in the watchlist at 13,067.

Interpretation

A low rate and a high breach count describe different operational problems.

Decision

Review rate, volume, and long waits together before prioritising provider support.

03 · Data quality

Can managers trust the refresh?

Why this visual exists. File status, record classification, reconciliation, and exceptions make the reporting boundary visible.

Finding

The refresh processed 25 files, flagged 44,044 records, and recorded zero unexpected rejections or revised periods.

Interpretation

A 66.4% acceptance rate is not hidden. Review-required records remain traceable by dataset and exception type.

Decision

Publish the governed view while routing unmatched organisation codes to review.
